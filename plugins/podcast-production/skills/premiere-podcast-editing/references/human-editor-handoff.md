# Handoff for human finishing

Use this when another editor will revise the native project. Keep the receiving editor's accepted conventions where they exist. A native file is only a useful handoff if its controls and source choices are easy to find.

## Make the working edit discoverable

- Save on the main editorial sequence and distinguish it from source/sync assemblies, backups and review exports. Use readable speaker, angle and phrase names; retain stable technical IDs in the manifest or metadata when automation needs them.
- Keep speaker colors consistent across the relevant timeline layers and supply a short legend. Separate camera identity from current speaker identity, especially on wide shots. Speaker-labeled portions of a shared mix must still say shared mix; colors and diarization do not establish isolated microphones.
- Put useful cue context beside the edit with readable clip names or markers: phrase, speaker and the applicable timeline/source clock. An internal JSON log can support this but should not be the only way to locate a spoken line.
- Identify where picture, grading, dialogue, word text and animation are edited. A silent alpha overlay is a valid delivery element; its linked AEP/comp provides individual text controls. Rendering a transparent graphic does not itself require replacing it with Dynamic Link.

## Preserve usable choices

Provide a synchronized camera assembly or native multicam source with all available alternatives, and a clear route from the selected edit to those alternatives. See [separate camera tracks](separate-camera-tracks.md) for the distinction between selected-shot tracks, trim handles and complete coverage. Keep dialogue routing independent when changing video angles.

Check the actual trailer and episode together for speaker-ID consistency. Use the same approved typography and animation template with framing-appropriate positions; a box visible in a work-in-progress frame is not approval of that style. If a replacement reference is promised but absent, retain the accepted treatment and identify that outstanding design decision.

Check the full episode's ending separately from the trailer's end card. Include the series outro/tag when the brief calls for it and approved assets exist; record a missing required ending rather than assuming the trailer card satisfies it or inventing new branding.

## Verify practical editability and deliver a short index

In a disposable sequence/project copy, locate a line using the visible names/cues, switch a representative shot to another synchronized angle, and adjust a title in its identified native source. Inspect the result and confirm the video switch leaves dialogue timing/routing unchanged. Protect the accepted edit and discard the trial copy after checking. If native access is unavailable, distinguish structural checks from this pending practical check.

Put a concise index at the top of the handoff: deliverable and priority, authoritative version/status, review link or local preview, editable project and exact sequence/comp, approved audio filename/version, and significant missing dependencies or unfinished work. Distinguish review media from editable sources. Keep source clocks and detailed manifests available without making the editor read them to find the main edit.

Capture subsequent editor feedback as a specific location, observed friction, change made and outcome. Reuse demonstrated improvements; do not promote tentative suggestions or unfinished designs to permanent defaults.
